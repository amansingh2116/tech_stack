\chapter{Finding Opportunities Beyond Job Postings}
\label{ch:beyond-postings}

One of the central recommendations across all the source material is not to limit your search to companies that
are currently advertising a role. Startups may be willing to consider an engineer who can demonstrate a useful
contribution even when a corresponding vacancy does not yet exist.

\section{Creating Your Own Opening}

A company might not have advertised an ML-infrastructure role, but a candidate could identify an opportunity to
reduce processing costs or improve reliability. If the candidate demonstrates that capability convincingly, the
founder may connect them with the relevant team or consider creating a position around what was demonstrated.

\begin{example}
One anecdote cited in the source material describes a student who reportedly secured a remote AI-engineering
position at the US-based company Pipe, with compensation described as approximately INR 60 LPA, after
consistently contacting US founders over time.
\end{example}

\begin{caution}
This is an anecdote used to illustrate the method, not independently verified. The transcript it comes from does
not confirm the person's employment, compensation, or the extent to which direct outreach alone (as opposed to
other factors) produced that outcome. Treat it the same way you'd treat any single success story: as evidence the
approach \emph{can} work, not as a guarantee that it \emph{will}.
\end{caution}

\section{The General Lesson}

Treat job descriptions as one source of opportunities, not the sole definition of where your skills could be
useful. This connects directly back to the Outreach Equation in Chapter~\ref{ch:outreach-engineering}: a
job-posting-only strategy artificially shrinks $N$ (the number of relevant prospects you can reach) to only the
companies that happen to be actively recruiting at the exact moment you're looking. Widening the aperture to
include companies with a \emph{plausible, demonstrable} problem --- whether or not they've posted a role --- is
one of the highest-leverage ways to grow $N$ without lowering message quality.
