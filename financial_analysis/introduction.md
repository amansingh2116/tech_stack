# Financial Analysis & Modeling: Course Notes

## 1. Introduction to Financial Analytics

Financial analytics is the systematic use of data analysis techniques to evaluate, interpret, and improve an organization’s financial health and performance. It transforms raw financial data into actionable insights for decision-makers.

### Core Importance

* **Understanding Business Performance:** Tracking revenue, expenses, and profitability to see where the company stands.
* **Risk Management:** Identifying potential financial threats (like liquidity issues or market volatility) before they become crises.
* **Strategic Growth:** Using historical data to forecast future trends and identify expansion opportunities.
* **Continuous Improvement:** Pinpointing inefficiencies in operations or capital allocation.
* **Investor & Stakeholder Confidence:** Providing transparent, data-driven reports to build trust with external parties.

## 3. Basic Accounting & Financial Statements

Before modeling, one must understand the "language of business." This module covers the foundational principles required to build financial reports from scratch.

* **Bookkeeping:** The systematic recording of daily financial transactions.
* **Financial Statements:** You will learn to construct the three primary reports:
  1. **Income Statement:** Summarizes revenue and expenses over a period.
  2. **Balance Sheet:** Reports assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time.
  3. **Cash Flow Statement:** Tracks the actual movement of cash in and out of the business.

## 4. Advanced Accounting Concepts

This section dives into complex accounting treatments that impact financial accuracy and business valuation.

* **Revenue Recognition:** Understanding *when* to record revenue (e.g., when earned, not just when cash is received).
* **Cost Recognition:** Applying the matching principle to align expenses with the revenue they generate.
* **Working Capital Management:**
* **Trade Receivables:** Money owed to the business by customers.
* **Trade Payables:** Money the business owes to its suppliers.
* **Inventory Turnover:** A ratio showing how many times a company has sold and replaced inventory during a specific period.

## 6. The Time Value of Money (TVM)

The core principle of finance is that **"a penny today is worth more than a penny in the future."**

### Why does money have a time value?

1. **Opportunity Cost:** Money held today can be invested to earn interest or capital gains.
2. **Inflation:** The purchasing power of money typically decreases over time.
3. **Risk:** Future payments carry the risk of not being received, whereas current cash is "in hand."

### Key Mathematical Concepts

* **Present Value ($PV$):** The current worth of a future sum of money.
* **Future Value ($FV$):** The value of a current asset at a specified date in the future.
* **Discount Rate ($r$):** The interest rate used to bring future cash flows back to the present.

$$PV = \frac{FV}{(1 + r)^n}$$

## 7. Financial Modeling

This is the culmination of the course: using the above concepts to build abstract representations of a business's financial future.

**Key Model Types:**

* **3-Statement Model:** Interlinking the Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statement.
* **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF):** Valuing a company based on the present value of its projected future cash flows.
* **Comparable Company Analysis (CCA):** Valuing a firm by comparing it to similar peer groups.
